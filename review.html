Review / From notes to next steps

Make time to notice.

A review is a chance to ask better questions about your work. Start with the entries themselves, then decide what deserves a closer look.

The intention

What were you trying to do?

Compare the original plan with what actually happened. A changed priority can explain an unfinished task better than a score can.

The conditions

What shaped the session?

Consider preparation, available time, distractions, and how focused you felt. Record observations without assuming they caused the outcome.

The response

What is worth trying again?

Choose a change you can describe and repeat. Leave enough context in your next entry to judge whether it helped.

An example of a useful review

Fictional example · No personal performance data

Two study sessions began with reading. Another began with a question and a short written answer. The question made it easier to identify what needed more attention.

A next step to test

Begin the next few study sessions with one question. Note whether this helps narrow the work, then compare the entries.

One observation is a starting point. A few comparable records give you more to work with.
